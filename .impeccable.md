## Design Context

### Users

College students using Goober Eats in two roles: **orderers** (students who want food from campus dining halls or GrubHub, paying below retail) and **swipers** (students with excess meal swipes or dining dollars, earning money by fulfilling orders). The orderer flow is mobile-first — someone in a dorm room or between classes, placing an order in under two minutes. The swiper flow is task-focused — checking a queue, accepting jobs, chatting, posting completion photos. Both audiences are gen-z, phone-native, and distrust anything that looks corporate or bloated.

**Primary context**: Mobile, casual, time-pressured. No patience for complexity.

### Brand Personality

**Slick, confident, minimal.**

Goober Eats sits in the fintech-adjacent lane: peer-to-peer money movement (meal swipes as currency), trust between strangers, real transactions. It should feel like Cash App or Robinhood — not a food delivery app, not a student startup. The "Goober" name is playful but the product is serious about making every interaction feel crisp and trustworthy.

The interface should feel like something a discerning student *chose* to use, not something handed to them by the university.

### Aesthetic Direction

**Light, clean, airy.** Warm off-white surfaces, nearly invisible structure, and the lime green accent kept rare enough to land with authority when it appears.

**Fonts:**
- Display/headings: **Bricolage Grotesque** — geometric, optically variable, editorial confidence without aggression. Heavy weights feel decisive.
- Body/UI: **Figtree** — clean geometric humanist, warm enough not to feel cold, tight enough not to feel soft.

**Color palette (OKLCH):**
- Background `--background` / `--color-bg`: `oklch(0.985 0.007 126)` — warm off-white tinted toward the brand hue
- Foreground `--foreground` / `--color-fg`: `oklch(0.12 0.008 126)` — near-black with a barely perceptible green tint
- Surface `--card` / `--color-surface`: `oklch(0.975 0.005 126)` — card/panel backgrounds
- Secondary text `--muted-foreground` / `--color-muted`: `oklch(0.50 0.018 126)` — muted but warm, not pure gray
- Border `--border` / `--color-border`: `oklch(0.89 0.010 126)` — tinted neutral, not gray
- **CTA base** `--primary`: `oklch(0.49 0.17 126)` — forest/hunter green; white text (~5.2:1, WCAG AA)
- **CTA press**: `oklch(0.44 0.16 126)` — deeper on active/press states (no dedicated token; apply via `hover:brightness-90` or component-level override)
- **CTA text** `--primary-foreground`: `oklch(0.985 0.007 126)` — warm white, high contrast on forest green
- **Lime flash** `--ring` / `--color-lime`: `oklch(0.77 0.17 126)` — the bright lime; used exclusively as hover ring / focus signal on primary CTAs. Never as a resting fill.

The entire neutral palette is tinted toward hue 126 (yellow-green). Even at chroma 0.007, this creates subconscious cohesion between the brand greens and all surfaces.

**The semantic shift from original palette:** Lime is no longer the resting CTA color — it is an *interaction reward*. The resting primary action is authoritative forest green (trust, stability). Lime flashes on hover as a kinetic signal that something will happen. This gives the interface two distinct modes: composed at rest, energetic in motion.

**Anti-references:** DoorDash, Uber Eats (too much color noise, consumer app chrome), anything that looks like a university portal.

### Design Principles

1. **Hierarchy over decoration** — Type ratios of 3:1 or greater between display and body. No decorative borders, no icon-over-heading patterns, no sparklines as filler.

2. **Lime is a verb, not a noun** — Lime green (`oklch(0.77 0.17 126)`) appears only as an interaction signal: the hover ring / focus state on primary CTAs. It never appears as a resting fill, an informational state, a secondary action hover, or decoration. The forest green is the noun (what the button is). The lime is the verb (what it's about to do).

3. **Speed signals trust** — Optimistic UI everywhere. Instant visual feedback. The interface should feel faster than it is. Skeletons over spinners. Committed state before server confirmation.

4. **Asymmetric structure** — Left-aligned content with intentional white space. No centered hero layouts. Think: a well-designed bank statement or payment receipt. Structured emptiness, not filled space.

5. **Minimal chrome** — The header is nearly invisible. No cards-inside-cards. No rounded icon-background squares above every heading. Surfaces blend into the background. Only structure that carries meaning gets to exist.
